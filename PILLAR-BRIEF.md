## H2 1 — Why Google Ads works for home improvement contractors
*Anchor the "hunting not fishing" hook. Establish intent advantage over social.*
- Search = declared intent. Someone typing "kitchen remodeler near me" is money-down-the-line, not top-of-funnel curiosity.
- Contrast with Facebook/Instagram: social *interrupts* people who weren't looking; search *answers* people already looking. Both have a place (foreshadows H2 2).
- High ticket + local + urgent = Google Ads' sweet spot. One booked $30k job pays for months of clicks.
- Kill the top objection early: "clicks are expensive" — yes, but frame against job value, not against clicks. (Sets up the ROI math in H2 5.)
- **Talking stat to include:** local service searches carry buyer intent far higher than social impressions — cite CPL-to-job-value ratio rather than raw CPC.

## H2 4 — The 5 pillars of a campaign that books jobs
*The meat. Use an H3 per pillar (or a bolded full-line label + paragraph). Actionable, not fluffy.*
- **1. Negative keywords** — cut "jobs," "salary," "DIY," "free," "how to." Where the budget leaks first.
- **2. Phrase & exact match, not broad** — broad match torches budget on junk searches; tight match = qualified clicks.
- **3. A dedicated landing page, not the homepage** — one trade, one offer, one form, above-the-fold CTA. Homepage traffic converts far worse.
- **4. Conversion tracking** — if you can't see which keyword produced the booked call, you're flying blind and can't cut losers.
- **5. Speed-to-lead** — contact within 5 minutes or the lead goes cold; the fastest contractor usually wins the job, not the cheapest.

## H2 5 — How much should you budget?
*Worked ROI math so the number feels earned, not plucked.*
- Start-from-goal method: pick a monthly job target → work backward through close rate → leads needed → CPL → monthly spend.
- Worked example (round numbers): want 2 jobs/mo → close ~1 in 4 leads → need ~8 leads → at ~$X CPL → ~$Y/mo ad spend → against $Z avg job value = clear positive ROAS.
- Set the floor honestly: below a real minimum monthly spend, Google can't gather enough data to optimize — underfunding is why most contractor campaigns "don't work."
- Ramp expectation: first 2–4 weeks = learning phase; judge on cost-per-booked-job over 60–90 days, not day-3 clicks.
